# Interview and Quality Guide

## Efficient questioning
Ask only questions whose answers materially affect the deck. Reuse answers from prior weeks, but confirm any week-specific changes. Prefer a short numbered list and allow the user to answer in matching numbers.

## Customer-facing voice
- Lead with customer outcomes, not internal process.
- Use plain language and define necessary technical terms.
- Avoid roadmap promises, competitive claims, unsupported superlatives, and internal acronyms.
- Convert features into customer value, but do not overstate source material.
- Use inclusive examples and neutral personas.

## Suggested timing model
Treat timing as a planning suggestion, not a sourced fact. A reasonable starting allocation is:
- 10% welcome and framing
- 60% instruction and demonstration
- 20% discussion or Q&A
- 10% recap and next steps
Adjust to the user's format.

## Speaker notes
Write notes as a natural facilitator script, not as slide duplication. Include transitions, demo cues, likely questions, and concise answers supported by the source. Prefix unsourced recommendations with “Facilitator suggestion:” or “Example:”.

## Accessibility and design
- Use 16:9 unless a template dictates otherwise.
- Use descriptive slide titles.
- Preserve strong contrast and readable type.
- Avoid relying on color alone.
- Add alt-text guidance for meaningful visuals when possible.
- Keep on-slide copy brief and move detail to notes.

## Quality gates
Pass only when factual accuracy, customer suitability, source traceability, readability, timing, filename, and loop state all validate. If one fails, revise before asking to continue.
